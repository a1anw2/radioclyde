# 📻 Leonard Cohen Show

**Duration:** 30 min
**Personas:** connor
**Segments:**

---

## Description

Half an hour spent entirely with one Leonard Cohen album. Connor picks a real
studio (or live) record from his catalog at random — never a compilation —
and plays a set of its songs, weighted toward the well-known ones but always
including at least one deeper cut, in shuffled order rather than album/track
order.

## Hosts

- **Connor**

## Track Selection

- Artist: Leonard Cohen.
- Pick one whole album of his at random.
- The whole show is built around that single album: every track comes from
  it, not a mix across several of his records.
- Never pick a "Greatest Hits", "Best Of", anthology, collection, or any
  other compilation release — it has to be a real studio or live album.
- No Christmas tracks.
- Favor the album's more popular, well-known songs, but make sure at least
  one lesser-known deep cut from the same record makes the cut too.
- Play the selected songs in random order, not album running order.

## Tone

Warm and a little reverent — like Connor's genuinely spending time with a
record he loves, not just running down a tracklist. Unhurried; this is a
quieter, more contemplative show than the station's upbeat rock hours.

## Track moves

Every track gets an `intro` (short teaser before it plays) and a `recap` (one
interesting fact after it ends) — both draw on that track's pre-fetched,
Wikipedia-sourced facts. Don't reuse the same fact in both: if it was spent on
the intro, the recap should say something else about the track. Since every
track shares one album, let intros and recaps build a running picture of the
record as a whole (its era, its making, how the songs relate) rather than
treating each track as a standalone fact — but the reveal that it's a
deep cut vs. a well-known single should come through in how it's introduced,
not as a flat label.
